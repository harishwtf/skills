# Visual Style Frameworks

Pre-built style directions to accelerate creative briefs. Mix and match elements across frameworks.

---

## Style 1: Bold Graphic (Ordinary Folk Energy)

**When to use:** Playful brands, consumer-facing SaaS, product launches that need energy and shareability.

**Visual Character:**
- Flat illustration with strategic depth (shadows, overlaps)
- Bold, saturated color palette (4-6 colors max)
- Strong geometric shapes — circles, rounded rectangles, clean curves
- Thick outlines optional — adds graphic punch
- Characters are stylized, not realistic — simple faces, expressive poses
- Backgrounds are color fields, not detailed environments

**Color Direction:**
- Primary: One bold hero color (electric blue, vivid coral, bright purple)
- Secondary: 2-3 complementary accents
- Neutrals: Near-black and near-white only
- No gradients on shapes — flat fills with shadow layers

**Typography:**
- Display: Geometric sans-serif (e.g., Satoshi, General Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans)
- Body: Clean sans-serif (e.g., Inter, DM Sans)
- Style: Large, bold headlines. Generous letter-spacing on labels.

**Motion Character:**
- Bouncy easing (overshoot on entries)
- Rhythmic timing synced to music
- Elements enter with personality — they bounce, spin, slide with attitude
- Snappy transitions — quick cuts with morphing shapes between scenes
- Scale and rotation used liberally

**Sound Direction:**
- Upbeat, percussive electronic or lo-fi
- Sound design: pops, clicks, whooshes — tactile and fun
- Music drives the edit — cuts land on beats

## Style 2: Warm Narrative (Giant Ant Energy)

**When to use:** Empathy-driven stories, B2B where the audience needs to see themselves, brand films, culture pieces.

**Visual Character:**
- Textured illustration — grain, paper texture, subtle imperfection
- Warm, muted palette with pops of color for emphasis
- Organic shapes — hand-drawn feeling, even if digital
- Characters are relatable — proportional, diverse, expressive faces
- Rich environments — workspaces, homes, cityscapes with detail and life
- Lighting is warm — golden hour feeling, soft shadows

**Color Direction:**
- Base: Warm neutrals (cream, warm gray, soft brown)
- Accents: Terracotta, sage green, dusty blue, muted gold
- Problem scenes: Cooler, desaturated
- Solution scenes: Warmer, more saturated
- Gradients: Subtle, atmospheric (sky-like)

**Typography:**
- Display: Rounded or humanist sans-serif (e.g., Nunito, Quicksand, Outfit)
- Body: Friendly and readable (e.g., Source Sans, Lato)
- Style: Moderate sizes, comfortable — not aggressive

**Motion Character:**
- Smooth, flowing easing (ease-in-out, gentle springs)
- Camera-like movement — pans, dollies, parallax depth
- Characters animate with weight and physics
- Transitions are environmental — camera moves through space
- Deliberate pacing — moments are allowed to breathe

**Sound Direction:**
- Acoustic instruments, piano, strings, gentle electronic
- Voiceover-friendly — music supports narration
- Sound design is subtle — ambient, atmospheric
- Emotional dynamics — quiet to full, mirroring the narrative arc

## Style 3: High Concept (Buck Energy)

**When to use:** Premium positioning, category-defining launches, complex concepts that need visual invention, keynote-worthy moments.

**Visual Character:**
- Mixed media — combine 3D, 2D, typography, photography, texture
- Unexpected material juxtapositions (paper + chrome, organic + digital)
- Abstract compositions — not literal illustration
- Bold art direction — every frame could be a poster
- Visual density — layers of meaning, reward repeat viewing
- Surreal or dreamlike logic — things transform, morph, defy physics

**Color Direction:**
- High contrast — deep darks against vivid accents
- Limited palette used with precision (2-3 colors plus black/white)
- Color has symbolic meaning (red = problem, blue = solution)
- Monochromatic sections with a single accent color
- Metallics, iridescence, or holographic for premium feel

**Typography:**
- Display: Distinctive, architectural (e.g., Clash Display, Cabinet Grotesk, Migra)
- Mono: For data or technical moments (e.g., JetBrains Mono, SF Mono)
- Style: Typography IS the design — oversized, cropped, layered, animated as a visual element

**Motion Character:**
- Cinematic easing — smooth, weighted, intentional
- Complex transformations — morphing, liquifying, assembling/disassembling
- Camera is a storytelling tool — dramatic angles, depth of field
- Slow moments of awe punctuated by rapid sequences
- Transitions are transformations — nothing just cuts, it *becomes*

**Sound Direction:**
- Cinematic score — synthesizers, bass, atmospheric pads
- Design-forward sound — every visual event has a sonic identity
- Tension and release structure
- Silence used as a power move

## Style 4: Kinetic Typography

**When to use:** Message-driven pieces, manifesto-style content, when the words ARE the visual, social-first content.

**Visual Character:**
- Text dominates the frame — it IS the visual
- Minimal illustration — shapes, lines, and color support the type
- High contrast backgrounds (black or white)
- Words animate as characters — they have personality and movement
- Scale shifts dramatize meaning — important words get BIG

**Color Direction:**
- Base: Black or very dark background
- Text: White primary, one accent color for emphasis words
- Accent: Single bold color (brand color or emotional color)
- Background accents: Subtle color shifts to mark sections

**Typography:**
- Multiple weights of one family for hierarchy (Thin to Black)
- Variable fonts for animated weight/width transitions
- Mix serif + sans for contrast moments
- ALL CAPS for impact, Mixed Case for narrative flow

**Motion Character:**
- Text drives timing — words appear in rhythm with VO or music
- Kinetic entrances: slam, type-on, reveal, glitch, scale-up
- Hold time proportional to word importance
- Camera zooms into and through text
- Rapid-fire sequences for energy, held frames for weight

## Style 5: Minimal Data (Modern SaaS)

**When to use:** Developer tools, analytics products, technical audiences, when showing capability without dumbing it down.

**Visual Character:**
- Clean, minimal, lots of negative space
- Data visualization as art — charts, graphs, nodes, connections
- UI elements abstracted — not literal screenshots but recognizable patterns
- Dot grids, line patterns, subtle geometric backgrounds
- Monochromatic with strategic color for data highlights
- Code snippets and terminal aesthetics where appropriate

**Color Direction:**
- Base: Near-black (#0a0a0a to #141414)
- Surface: Dark gray (#1a1a1a to #262626)
- Text: Off-white (#e5e5e5)
- Accent: One or two neon/bright accents (cyan #00D4FF, green #00FF88, or purple #8B5CF6)
- Data colors: Small, precise palette for chart elements

**Typography:**
- Display: Geometric sans (e.g., Geist, Inter, Manrope)
- Mono: For code, data, technical labels (e.g., Geist Mono, Fira Code)
- Style: Clean, precise, well-tracked — not decorative

**Motion Character:**
- Precise easing — no bounce, no overshoot
- Data animates with purpose — numbers count up, charts draw, connections form
- Transitions are structural — panels slide, sections reveal
- Cursor interactions — show the product being used (abstracted)
- Particle systems or node graphs for connection/network concepts

**Sound Direction:**
- Minimal electronic — clean synths, subtle clicks
- Data sonification — sounds mapped to visual events
- Quiet confidence — not trying too hard
- Ambient pads for atmosphere
